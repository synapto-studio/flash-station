    Put it on a board

    1. On the Mac, open Terminal and log in to the Pi: ssh USERNAME@sotapi.localThe prompt changes to end in @sotapi:~ $. That's the Pi.
    2. Connect the board the usual way: 3.3 V, GND, the 2.2 kΩ on TX and RX to the UPDI pad, nothing in its USB.Or plug it into one of the Pi's USB ports, take the 3.3 V wire off, and hold just the UPDI lead on the UPDI pad.
    3. Copy this and paste it into the Pi's terminal. It carries every change you've made. Press Enter. It takes under a minute.

    How close is this to the drive? The lighting, the tempo grid and the loudness meter are the firmware's own code, ported line for line and checked frame for frame against it. The mic is modelled as a typical analogue MEMS part (−38 dBV/Pa) and the room as a level, without echo or the case's muffling, so treat the dB figures as a guide until they've been checked in a real room; micTrimDb corrects them. The demo is a plain two-step: kicks on 1 and the "and" of 3, snares on 2 and 4, with a 4-bar breakdown.
