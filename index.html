Flash Station

Open this page in Chrome or Edge on your computer. Safari and Firefox can't talk to USB devices, so nothing can be flashed from here. You can still look around and use the simulated board.

Ready

Pick the board on the jig to start.

  1. 1Board on the jig

    3V5V

    Set the UPDI Friend switch to 3V before the pogo pin touches the board. Never 5V on Pulse: its UPDI pad feeds the mic's 3.3 V rail.

  2. 2Programmer

    Plug the UPDI Friend into the computer, then choose its port. After that it connects by itself whenever it is plugged in.

    Speed

  3. 3Check the board

    Board powered from its USB plug, pogo pin on the UPDI pad.

    • 4Engine and lighting

    • 5Drive details

    • Erases the chip, writes the engine with its lighting, sets the BOOTEND fuse, then writes the ID card. Everything is read back to check it.

      Terminal fallback

      If the browser flasher misbehaves, the same job runs from the Mac's Terminal with pymcuprog (install once: pip3 install pymcuprog).

    Song moments
    
        
    A Pulse can recognise a moment in a song from what its mic hears, and play an animation right there, like a white flash on the drop. Add a clip of the song, mark the bars the drive listens for and when the animation plays, check it on the drive, then save it.
    
        
    Song moments are for Pulse drives: Core has no microphone. Choose Pulse at the top of the tab.
    
        
    
          
    
            
            
            
            
          
    
          
    The clip is copied into the library's songs folder. It stays on this computer and is never uploaded.
    
          
          
          
    Click where the animation should start: Play at goes to the nearest beat, and the drive listens to the bars before it. Drag the orange band to listen somewhere else, or the orange line to play later. Play at can't go before "recognised by here". Bar numbers are along the top.

    Light cues
    
        
    Make a song moment's animation by playing along. Press Record cues: the song open in Song moments plays from a little before Play at, and each key you press adds a light cue there. Hold a key for as long as the cue should last. Then play it back, change any cue's LEDs, colour and brightness, and save it as the moment's animation.
    
        
    Light cues are for song moments, so they're for Pulse drives. Choose Pulse at the top of the tab.
    
        
    
          
    Open or add a song in Song moments above first. The cues start at its Play at.
    
          
    
          
    Keys 1–4 pick the LEDs for the next cues (all, top, middle, bottom). Z takes out the last cue, Esc stops.

    Colour sets
    
        
    Save the colours above under a name, and use them again later. Each set is for one board, so Core shows Core sets and Pulse shows Pulse sets. They are kept in the library folder.
    
        
    
          
          
          
          
        
    
        
    
        
    Phase files
    
        
    A phase file saves the settings for one part of the show: load-up, standard or beat. Saving under a name that exists makes a new version and keeps the old one. A routine (below) picks one file per phase. Colours are not in phase files: use colour sets for those. Recognition has no settings yet.
    
        
    
          
          
          
        
    
        
    
          
          
        
    
        
    
        
    Routines
    
        
    A routine picks one phase file for each phase, a colour set and a brightness. Leave a phase on "Engine default" to keep the engine's own look. "Newest" follows new versions of a phase file; pick a version to pin it. Saving under an existing name replaces that routine. Flash Station and Initialise list routines by name.
    
        
    
        
    
          
          
        
    
        
    
          
          
          
          
        
    
        
    
        
    Screen colours are only a guide: the LEDs' green is much stronger than a monitor's, so judge yellow and orange on the real drive. These settings are not saved or sent yet.
